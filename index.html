Machine learning for the human heart.

I'm Tanay — a computer science student at Carnegie Mellon and a published ML researcher. I build transformer models that synthesize diagnostic-quality ECG signals, with two first-authored papers in IEEE venues.

About me

I grew up in Portland, Oregon, and started doing research in high school — mostly asking one question: can we generate the parts of a 12-lead ECG we didn't record, well enough for a doctor to trust? Chasing that question led to first-authored papers at IEEE EMBC and IEEE SENSORS, presentations at the Heart Rhythm Society and AI Med, and two ISEF Grand Awards.

Now I'm studying computer science at Carnegie Mellon (Class of 2030). Along the way I've interned as an ML engineer at Hello Alfred.ai, captained a FIRST Tech Challenge robotics team for four years, and run a small dessert business, TnT Treats, since 2019. In 2026 I was named a Coca-Cola Scholar, one of 150 selected from over 110,000 applicants.

Research & projects

The main thread of my research is ECG synthesis: a clinical ECG needs twelve leads and ten electrodes, and I ask how much of that record a model can recover from a much smaller set of measurements — the kind a wearable could realistically capture. That question runs through most of the work below, though not all of it: IntelliCane took the same deep learning toolkit to assistive navigation.

  1. 2025
    Transformer-Based Framework with Dynamic Multiscale Attention Mechanisms for 12-Lead ECG Synthesis
    47th IEEE EMBC · Full paper · IEEE Xplore & PubMed

    First-authored and presented at the world's largest biomedical engineering conference. The model attends across several time resolutions at once, so it can hold onto both the sharp QRS complex and the slower repolarization it sits inside.

    Project page → Paper (PDF) Slides (PDF)
  2. 2025
    A Morphologically-Aware Transformer Network for High-Fidelity V1–V5 ECG Lead Generation
    24th IEEE Sensors · Full paper · IEEE Xplore

    First-authored and presented at IEEE SENSORS. The network is trained on waveform morphology, not just point-wise error, so the generated V1–V5 chest leads keep the shape features clinicians actually read.

    Project page → Paper (PDF) Poster (PDF)
  3. 2025
    IntelliCane: A Deep Learning Cane for Active Obstacle Avoidance & Navigation for the Visually Impaired
    Regeneron ISEF 2025 · Grand Award, 3rd place · AAAI Special Award

    A cane with an onboard deep learning system that detects obstacles and guides the user around them in real time — the same toolkit as my ECG work, aimed at assistive navigation instead.

    Project page → Abstract (PDF) Poster (PDF)
