Research & projects

My main research focuses on ECG synthesis. A clinical ECG needs twelve leads and ten electrodes; my work synthesizes a diagnostic-quality 12-lead ECG from a subset of leads that a low-cost wearable can record. That makes at-home monitoring and early detection of cardiovascular disease easier and more accessible. It also lets low-resource regions get high-quality ECGs without the need for expensive ECG recording devices. Outside this line of research, I've also built IntelliCane, an autonomously navigating cane that actively guides the visually impaired.

  1. 2025
    Transformer-Based Framework with Dynamic Multiscale Attention Mechanisms for 12-Lead ECG Synthesis
    47th IEEE EMBC · First-author full paper · IEEE Xplore & PubMed

    I first-authored and presented this paper at the world's largest biomedical engineering conference. I created a custom transformer that uses dynamic scaling windows to capture both the sharp spikes and the subtle waves of a heartbeat, allowing for accurate ECG synthesis. The model isn't limited to ECGs, either — it can be fitted to synthesize any type of waveform.

  2. 2025
    A Morphologically-Aware Transformer Network for High-Fidelity V1–V5 ECG Lead Generation
    24th IEEE Sensors · First-author full paper · IEEE Xplore

    I first-authored and presented this paper at IEEE's flagship sensor conference. Where my EMBC model is general-purpose, this multi-stage transformer is customized to ECGs: the architecture builds in the specific features and complexities of heart waveforms — the P, QRS, and T segments — making it highly accurate at synthesizing them.

  3. 2025
    IntelliCane: A Deep Learning Cane for Active Obstacle Avoidance & Navigation for the Visually Impaired
    Regeneron ISEF 2025 · Grand Award, 3rd place · AAAI Special Award

    I built the first goal-driven, self-navigating aid for the visually impaired. IntelliCane can interpret destinations in plain language — like “go to a water fountain” — and its camera-based AI agents detect obstacles, find a walkable path, and actively guide the user there with servo steering and spoken directions.

  4. 2024
    Validation of Einthoven and Goldberger's Electrocardiographic Lead Systems
    Heart Rhythm Society 2024 · Abstract & poster

    I re-validated the century-old formulas that modern electrocardiography is built on, comparing their computed leads against real recordings across a range of heart conditions — average errors came out under 0.3%. I presented the work at the Heart Rhythm Society's annual meeting, showing how essential these formulas are for detecting more types of cardiovascular disease from a wearable device with a limited lead set.

    Project page → Abstract (PDF) Poster (PDF)
  5. 2024
    Synthesis of a Diagnostic-Caliber 12-Lead ECG Using a Novel Multi-resolution Transformer
    Regeneron ISEF 2024 · HRX 2024 · Grand Award, 2nd place

    I built my first end-to-end transformer pipeline for generating a diagnostic-quality 12-lead ECG from three recordable leads, pairing the mathematical lead-computation formulas with a multi-resolution neural network personalized to each individual. This is the project that launched my research, and I later presented it at HRX 2024.

    Project page → Abstract (PDF) Poster (PDF)
  6. 2023
    Early Detection and Synthesis of a 12-Lead ECG Using Density-Based Clustering and GANs
    AI Med 2023 · Youngest of 80+ presenters

    I trained a GAN to generate a 12-lead ECG from the three leads a smart device can record, then used clustering against a person's own ECG history to flag early signs of heart disease without the need for pre-labeled data. Because the baseline is the person's own heart, it works even for people with pre-existing conditions, where standard classifiers fall short. I presented this work at AI Med 2023 as the youngest of 80+ pre- and post-doctoral presenters.
