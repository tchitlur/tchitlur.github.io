Standard 12-lead layout  ·  25 mm/s
Recorded — 3 leads Reconstructed — 9 leads

An illustration of the problem I work on, not patient data. A clinical ECG needs twelve leads and ten electrodes. My research asks how much of that record a model can recover from a much smaller set of measurements — the kind a wearable could realistically capture.

I started working on electrocardiogram synthesis in high school and have kept at it for four years. The through-line is a single question: which parts of a twelve-lead ECG are redundant, and which carry diagnostic information that cannot be inferred from anywhere else?

That work has become three peer-reviewed papers, two Regeneron ISEF Grand Awards, and talks at IEEE EMBC, IEEE SENSORS, the Heart Rhythm Society, and AI-Med. I am now a first-year computer science student at Carnegie Mellon.

A Morphologically-Aware Transformer Network for High-Fidelity V1–V5 ECG Lead Generation

24th IEEE SENSORS · Full paper · IEEE Xplore

Precordial leads carry most of the information a cardiologist uses to localize ischemia, and they are the hardest to place. This network is trained to preserve wave morphology rather than only minimize point-wise error, which is what makes a synthetic lead readable instead of merely close.
