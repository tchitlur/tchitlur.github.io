Slipstream: Rare-Disease Research Navigator

Hack-Nation Global AI Hackathon · 2nd place of 3,000+ · October 3–4, 2026

There are thousands of rare diseases, and most families affected by one are starting from scratch. But many rare diseases break the same way: the same kind of lost protein, the same mechanism, the same path from gene discovery to a treatment. Slipstream lets a parent type in their child's gene or diagnosis and see which disease community is further along that same road, and what can be borrowed from it.

I built a 2,700-node disease graph from Gene2Phenotype, the Human Phenotype Ontology, ClinicalTrials.gov, NIH RePORTER, and PubMed, clustered it with Louvain community detection, and ran an LLM pipeline over 1,000+ studies and trials. For each condition, Slipstream computes a research-readiness ladder (gene link, mechanism, patient organization, registry, disease models, funding, targeted trial, approved therapy). For any neighboring disease, it gives a transfer verdict per asset: transferable, needs expert review, or do not transfer. The output is a one-page sourced brief a family can send to a patient community. It covers 83 conditions across 65 genes in developmental and epileptic encephalopathies in depth, plus 2,783 more conditions at mechanism-and-symptoms depth.

The Hack-Nation Global AI Hackathon is a 24-hour, worldwide hackathon hosted with the MIT Club of Northern California and the MIT Club of Germany, with 15+ in-person hubs including MIT, Harvard, Stanford, Berkeley, and Carnegie Mellon, plus online participation. The 7th edition ran October 3–4, 2026, with 3,000+ participants competing across sponsor challenges for $30K+ in prizes, judged by mentors from OpenAI, Meta, Apple, and leading AI startups. Slipstream was built for Challenge 05, “AI Atlas for the World's Rare Diseases,” and took 2nd place.
