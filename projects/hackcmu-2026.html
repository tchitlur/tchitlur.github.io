GlucoPilot: A Personalized Glucose Simulator for Type 1 Diabetes

HackCMU 2026 · Carnegie Mellon · 2nd place, Food track · September 11–12, 2026

Living with Type 1 diabetes means making dozens of small decisions a day about food, drink, insulin, and activity, with only a rough feel for how they interact. GlucoPilot is a personalized simulation of a Type 1 diabetic's body: food, alcohol, caffeine, insulin, exercise, sleep, illness, stress, and water all feed a physiology model, and the app shows what happens next.

It produces a short-horizon glucose forecast with uncertainty bands, makes permission decisions (“can I have another drink?”) together with the condition that would make it safe, and runs 30-day comparisons of lifestyle changes side by side. Under the hood it combines the Hovorka glucose-insulin model with a LightGBM residual model, in a FastAPI backend with a React and Recharts front end; meals can be logged by text or photo via a vision-language model. All data is synthetic, and it is decision support, not medical advice.

HackCMU is Carnegie Mellon's annual 24-hour, beginner-friendly hackathon, organized by ACM@CMU. The 2026 edition, “Midnight Express,” ran September 11–12, 2026, on campus in Pittsburgh; tracks were announced at the opening ceremony and teams chose one at submission. GlucoPilot took 2nd place in the Food track.
